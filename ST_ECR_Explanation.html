ST-ECR v4: The Four-Route Paradigm

Timescale Separation & Catastrophic Forgetting Defense

The Mechanics: Four Levels of Epistemic Routing

Route 1: Memory Retrieval

Computation Control: When draft σep is high, the model queries historical latent trajectories from the episodic memory bank to gain context on rare events.

Route 2: Conformal Abstention

Decision Control: When σep > ω (rolling quantile), the gate triggers a hard "do not trade" signal to protect capital from extreme uncalibrated uncertainty.

Route 3: Position Sizing

Sizing Control: σep dynamically acts as a risk discount λ in the Kelly criterion formula, smoothly shrinking the allocation weighting as uncertainty rises.

Route 4: Online Update

Self-Improvement: When σep > τonline, the adapter freezes the encoder and initiates gradient steps strictly on the ensemble heads using recent data.

1. The Financial Perspective (Market Regimes & Risk Management)

The Problem: Financial markets are non-stationary; they undergo sudden, violent regime shifts (e.g., a macro shock or flash crash).

When the market breaks its historical pattern, the model's epistemic uncertainty (σep) spikes. From a portfolio management perspective, the model immediately plays defense: Route 2 (Abstention) and Route 3 (Position Sizing) pull capital out of the market. However, a fund cannot sit in cash forever.

Route 4 (Online Update) acts as the fund's internal quant team: it uses the recent chaotic window of data to recalibrate its expectations.

By only updating the ensemble "heads", the model learns the new short-term volatility and correlation regime (the "current weather") while completely preserving its deep structural knowledge of asset fundamentals (the "long-term climate") stored in the frozen encoder.

2. The Spatio-Temporal Perspective (Manifold Preservation)

The Problem: Spatio-temporal architectures require massive amounts of historical data to learn the complex, multi-asset topological graphs and temporal dynamics.

The ST-Encoder projects sequences into a very specific latent geometry. If we updated the whole network on new data, we would warp this latent space, destroying the model's ability to match current patterns to historical trajectories (Route 1 Memory Retrieval).

Timescale Separation locks the spatial and temporal parameters in place. We view the ST-Encoder as capturing the fundamental "physics" of the asset universe. Even during a crash, these underlying physics don't change; only the immediate mapping from those physics to expected returns and variance changes. Route 4 updates this mapping without destroying the beautifully crafted space-time manifold.

3. The Online Learning Perspective (The Stability-Plasticity Dilemma)

The Problem: In continual learning, models face the "stability-plasticity dilemma." If a model is too plastic, it suffers from catastrophic forgetting (overwriting old knowledge with recent noise). If it's too stable, it fails to adapt to concept drift.

ST-ECR solves this through a dual mechanism:

  • Trigger-Gated Plasticity: Unlike Test-Time Training (which stupidly updates on every sample), Route 4 is self-referential. It only fires when σep > τonline. The model literally uses its own ignorance as the mathematical trigger to self-improve, making it both computationally efficient and resilient to overfitting on normal noise.
  • Structural Stability: By mathematically severing the gradients to the encoder (∂ℒ / ∂θenc = 0), we achieve perfect timescale separation. The slow feature-extractor remains perfectly stable (zero catastrophic forgetting), while the fast ensemble heads remain highly plastic to resolve the immediate distribution shift.

The Elevator Pitch for a Reviewer

"ST-ECR v4 treats epistemic uncertainty as a self-referential control signal. When the model doesn't recognize a market regime, it uses Routes 2 and 3 to stop trading so it doesn't lose money, while simultaneously triggering Route 4 to actively learn the new regime. And it does this via strict timescale separation, adapting its predictive heads to the new environment without categorically forgetting the historical spatial-temporal structures it learned offline."
